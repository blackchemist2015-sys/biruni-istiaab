استيعاب الوجوه الممكنة في صنعة الأسطرلاب al-Bīrūnī's treatise on the astrolabe, every figure redrawn

Each hand-drawn figure of the manuscript is set beside a modern figure built from the geometry the text describes: intersections, perpendiculars, projections and ratios, computed rather than traced. The manuscript's own letters (ا ب ج د ه و ز …) stay where the copyist put them, so the reconstruction can be read against the Arabic text line by line.

Colour convention
Red for construction lines, black for the finished, engraved lines, as in the manuscript.
Parameters
Obliquity 23;35° (al-Bīrūnī's value). Where the text fixes no latitude, a typical one is chosen and named in the figure's note.
Verification
260 numerical checks confirm the constructions: points collinear, circles through the stated points, lengths equal where the text says so.
Where text and drawing differ
The geometry of the text is followed, the manuscript's letters are kept, and the note says so.

الصفيحة الآفاقية (الوجه الأول: العروض ١–٣٣)

عمل الصفيحة الآفاقية

الصفيحة الآفاقية (universal plate of horizons), first face. Every horizon passes through two opposite points of the equator. Four 'origins' are used - the ends of the two diameters on the equator - and the plate is turned a quarter for each successive degree of latitude, so that the horizons issuing from any one origin differ by 4°: left 0 (the east-west diameter itself), 4, 8 ... 32; top 1, 5 ... 33; right 2, 6 ... 30; bottom 3, 7 ... 31. Only the parts between the two tropics are kept. Each half-diameter between the tropics is divided into degrees of declination (every 2°, numbered by tens), north inside the equator and south outside it, by (unmarked) day-circles.

الساعات الزمانية على ظهر الاصطرلاب

معرفة الارتفاع من الساعات وعكسه بطريق صناعي (ثلاثة أشكال)

معرفة ذلك بطريق صناعي

معرفة الارتفاع من الساعات وعكسه بطريق صناعي (three analemma figures). Circle ا ب ج د = meridian, ب ه د = horizon, ز ه ح = equator (arc ب ز = latitude). Given the altitude (arc ز ل), ل م is drawn parallel to ز ح and م س parallel to ب د. (a) equinox: arc س ب is the time elapsed since sunrise. (b, c) other declinations: arc ا ط = the declination; ط ف ي parallel to ا ه ج; ط ي is the 'sine of the day' (جيب النهار). عنكبوت الاصطرلاب المطبل (the 'drum' rete). The northern ecliptic (centre on the meridian between Capricorn and Aries) and the southern ecliptic (centre on the opposite side) are both drawn; they cut each other at the two equinoxes. عنكبوت الاصطرلاب المسرطن

الاصطرلاب المسرطن

عنكبوت الاصطرلاب المسرطن (the 'crab' rete, ascribed to Bastulus). From the northern ecliptic the spring and autumn quadrants are taken, from the southern ecliptic the summer and winter quadrants. The quarters join at the equinoxes (both at the equator) but at the solstices the northern and southern pieces end on different circles (Cancer's on the rim and on the inner circle), and are joined by the east-west bar. The stars of each quarter of the rete are placed by that quarter's own projection. (Oriented as in the manuscript: Libra up, Aries down.)

الاصطرلاب المسرطن وسائر المزاجات

ستة أنواع من العناكب الممزوجة (al-Sijzi's six mixed retes, copied by Biruni): الثوري، البوري، الباطي، الصدفة، السلحفي، الجاموسي. Each is built from sign-arcs taken from the northern ecliptic or from the southern ecliptic; a northern sign lies outside the equator only in the southern projection and a southern sign only in the northern one, so the outer/inner position of each piece in the manuscript decides its projection. Arcs are exact; the distribution of the signs follows the legible labels of the manuscript (partly conjectural where the drawing is illegible); the animal ornaments are omitted.
